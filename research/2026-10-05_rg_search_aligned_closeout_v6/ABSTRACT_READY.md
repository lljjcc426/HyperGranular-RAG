## Initial version, prepared before new experiments

Title: **When Set Scores Fail to Select Evidence: A Controlled Study of Budgeted Multi-hop RAG**

Selecting a small evidence set for multi-hop question answering requires balancing support coverage against a reader's context budget. Learned set scores offer a way to represent interactions between passages, but their behavior on constructed training sets need not transfer to the sets found by search. We study this gap with shared dense candidates, a fixed reader, and a 1,024-token input budget. First-, second-, and fourth-order factorization models and a general permutation-invariant set model receive the same support-annotation supervision. On an exposed development panel of 128 HotpotQA and MuSiQue questions, the fourth-order model achieves a mean answer F1 of 0.4171 across three training seeds, compared with 0.4055 for dense selection and 0.4303 for maximal marginal relevance. Strong performance on constructed set comparisons therefore does not establish a downstream advantage over a simple diversity baseline. Exact granular-ball and k-means search preserve flat-search selections, yet reduce inner products without reducing measured search time at 128 candidates. These findings separate proxy fit, evidence selection, answer quality, and computational cost, and motivate evaluating learned objectives on the states their search procedures actually visit. The results are development evidence, not an independent confirmation or a claim of general retrieval superiority.

## Completed-v6 version, for human use after closeout

Title unchanged. This version includes only completed experiments; it does not replace or backdate an abstract-registration receipt.

Selecting complementary evidence under a limited context budget requires a score that remains useful on the sets found by search. We examine this requirement with first-, second-, and fourth-order factorization models and a permutation-invariant set model, sharing dense candidates and support-annotation supervision. Strong performance on constructed set pairs does not translate into stronger evidence selection: the static fourth-order model loses complete annotated support relative to a size-matched dense baseline. A bounded correction trains the deployed score on actual search frontiers and final sets, with an equal-update static replay control. For the primary seed, fourth-order checkpoint-panel completeness increases from 0.4531 to 0.5156, but answer F1 on a separate 128-question development panel falls from 0.4181 to 0.3766, below maximal marginal relevance's 0.4303. In both training seeds, every aligned model remains below this baseline in F1 point estimate. Exact granular-ball and k-means indexes preserve flat-search selections but do not accelerate the measured 128-candidate search. The study distinguishes proxy fit, selection quality, answer quality, and runtime, identifying where additional model structure does and does not provide practical value. All learned-selector comparisons are development evidence, rather than a new independent confirmation.
