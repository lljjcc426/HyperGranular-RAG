Title: **When Set Scores Fail to Select Evidence: A Controlled Study of Budgeted Multi-hop RAG**

Selecting a small evidence set for multi-hop question answering requires balancing support coverage against a reader's context budget. Learned set scores offer a way to represent interactions between passages, but their behavior on constructed training sets need not transfer to the sets found by search. We study this gap with shared dense candidates, a fixed reader, and a 1,024-token input budget. First-, second-, and fourth-order factorization models and a general permutation-invariant set model receive the same support-annotation supervision. On an exposed development panel of 128 HotpotQA and MuSiQue questions, the fourth-order model achieves a mean answer F1 of 0.4171 across three training seeds, compared with 0.4055 for dense selection and 0.4303 for maximal marginal relevance. Strong performance on constructed set comparisons therefore does not establish a downstream advantage over a simple diversity baseline. Exact granular-ball and k-means search preserve flat-search selections, yet reduce inner products without reducing measured search time at 128 candidates. These findings separate proxy fit, evidence selection, answer quality, and computational cost, and motivate evaluating learned objectives on the states their search procedures actually visit. The results are development evidence, not an independent confirmation or a claim of general retrieval superiority.
